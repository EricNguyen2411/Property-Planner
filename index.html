How to use this app
The buying process

What actually happens, roughly in order, once you decide to buy.

1
Get a real sense of your borrowing power
Before looking at anything, know roughly what a bank will lend you. A pre-approval isn't a guarantee — the bank can still decline once it values the actual property — but it stops you wasting time on properties you can't afford.
Afford tab
2
Search, and sanity-check every serious option
For an investment property, check the numbers actually work before you get attached — rent estimate, yield, and whether you'll be topping it up out of pocket each week.
Invest tab
3
Make an offer or bid
Private sale: you negotiate a price, usually with a small holding deposit. Auction: the highest bid over reserve wins, unconditionally, on the spot — there's no cooling-off period at auction in most states, so all your checks need to happen before auction day, not after.
4
Building & pest inspection
Get this done before you're locked in, not after. It's a few hundred dollars to find out about structural issues, pests, or major defects that could cost tens of thousands — skipping it to move faster is one of the most common regrets first-time buyers report.
5
Cooling-off period (private sales only)
Most states give you a short window (commonly 3-5 business days, but it varies — confirm the exact period for your state and contract) after signing to pull out, usually for a small penalty. Auctions typically have no cooling-off at all.
6
Exchange contracts & pay your deposit
Once cooling-off passes (or you buy at auction), contracts become binding and your deposit — commonly 10%, sometimes less — is paid, usually held in trust until settlement.
7
Conveyancing & finance finalisation
Your conveyancer or solicitor handles title checks and contract details; your lender finalises formal loan approval and organises the funds for settlement.
8
Settlement
Typically 30-90 days after exchange. Funds transfer, you get the keys, and stamp duty plus all the other upfront costs are paid around this point.
Costs tab
Cooling-off periods, deposit conventions and exact settlement timing vary by state and by contract — this is a general guide, not legal advice. Always have a conveyancer or solicitor review your specific contract.
Glossary
Stamp duty brackets, first-home-buyer thresholds and LMI premiums are set by state governments and lenders, and change often — sometimes every financial year. Figures here are indicative and last verified . Always confirm exact numbers with your state revenue office, lender, or conveyancer before relying on them.
